## Scenarios
Each session is expected to be episodic, encompassing a single primary scenario. That scenario might also be one of the stepping stones of a full-on campaign or multiple simultaneous mini-scenarios.

Each scenario has a defining dramatic question that, once answered, brings the scenario unavoidably to its conclusion, also bringing the session to its end. 

Answering this dramatic question favorably is the primary objective of the player characters. How this ends, and the adventure the players commit to next, helps the Facilitator get ready the next scenario which will have relevant structural and game elements as well as prepared material for guided improvisation.

Encounters are similar to scenarios in this way - they each also have a dramatic question that once answered in play, proves the results of that encounter and sets the stage for how the next encounter starts.
## Session Start Procedure
Ideally, players arrive close to the agreed-upon time. It is good for each member of the table to be welcomed, wanted, and to have some time to get re-acquainted, socially warm-up, and relax. Some side chatter can even be good for the game, during the game. As the Facilitator you should relax, but also be mindful and implement the structure of the game.

1. Each member of the table is welcomed as they arrive. Some time is portioned for warming up and getting ready to play.
2. Before long the session is brought to an unambiguous start. 
3. The Facilitator recaps relevant details from past adventures to help ready each players mind for this scenario, at least including how things ended last session and what the players said they'd be doing next. 
4. The Facilitator introduces the first scene or area of the current scenario - the skies should help narrate weather and time, the surroundings should include key details about the environment and activity in the area, and the hook should provide a clear invitation to act.
## Session Play Loop
Your game leader will present opportunities to engage with the world. As you do, quests will reveal themselves that will include opportunities to connect, explore, fight, and solve. 

Most of the game follows a simple pattern:

1. The Facilitator re-hashes what is important or urgent in the current moment.
2. The players ask questions, investigate, explore, and declare actions. 
3. The Facilitator adjudicates actions. When appropriate the dice are rolled. The roll total helps determine the results and the game leader translates those into the narrative.
4. The Facilitator describes how things change and transitions the focus to the next moment of opportunity.
## Session End Procedure
As the session is brought to a close, the table answers these questions together:

1. What happened?
2. What do we want to remember? What did we learn? Make note of these things.
3. What are we doing next in the game?

**Assigning XP**
Next, the GM assigns equal XP to each party member. They gain 1 XP when the scenario is brought to a close. This is the completion of the quest, for better or for worse. Answering it victoriously grants 1 additional XP. This is completing it well. Further XP can be gained under the following conditions based on your GM’s feeling:

- If you suffered no major losses, gain 1 XP
- If you found optional content, gain 1 XP
- If you completed optional scenario objectives, gain 1 XP
- If you exemplified teamwork, gain 1 XP
## Pre-Prep The Next Session
Before the players each go their separate ways, the table spends a few moments scheduling in the most likely dates for the next two gaming sessions and grants the Facilitator permission to follow-up with each member of the table in advance of those dates.

The table may also briefly discuss other plans or back-up plans as necessary, including sub-plots, other games for game night, budget-friendly local real-life activities, serving, uplifting, or celebrating someone at the table, or rescheduling an upcoming session.

**Taking Session Notes**
Finally, the Facilitator takes simple notes on the scenario:

- Bullet point events from the session. What happened and when? Or at least, what order?
- Bullet point NPCs from the session. Who did the players meet (what are they known as, and what “character background” phrase best describes them) and where? 
- What can you bring full circle later (sooner or later) by bringing rewards and consequences back around to the players directly due to this session?

These notes, as well as clearly knowing what the player characters will be doing next are used by the Facilitator later for planning the next session.
